The Art of Play

Tiny pieces.
Whole worlds.

Art you can hold in your hands.

Illustration

Before anyone pulled up a chair, someone painted the world. The lid, the board and every card face are drawn to be read across a table, under a warm lamp, upside down.

Sculpture

A crown you can turn between two fingers. Every piece is shaped to be known at a glance, lifted without looking and held for a whole evening.

Writing

The rulebook is a short story told in steps. It has to teach, end arguments and set a mood, and the whole world lives between its lines.

Systems

Luck and choice, weighed by hand. A good system makes each decision feel like yours and gives everyone a reason to stay seated.

The rules stay put.
The story doesn’t.

House Rules is made by KITE.STUDIO, a small studio for the web. A good game keeps you at the table a little longer. We build websites that do the same.

  1. 01

    Start with the point of view.

    Every project begins with what it has to say: its character, its audience, its reason to exist. Here that meant treating each game as a small work of art.

  2. 02

    Leave room for discovery.

    Type, image, material and motion are tried side by side, and every experiment has to make the idea clearer. Here, an engraved and gilded world you can step into.

  3. 03

    Build the feeling.

    Design and code stay in one conversation, down to the timing of a reveal and the weight of a gesture. Here, cards that deal and paint that opens a room.

  4. 04

    Care all the way through.

    The finish lives where it is least likely to be noticed: a line break on a phone, a keyboard path, a gentler motion setting. Here, from the first screen to the last.
